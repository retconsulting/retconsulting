Weekly recaps

  1. W31 2026 27 July to 2 August 2026

    The cheap tier got cheap enough to change plans

     4 briefs

    Pricing, access and open weights carried the week, and raw capability barely moved. Copilot opened its document agents to a choice of model, Gemini and OpenAI both cut what a token costs, and DeepSeek put a competitive checkpoint under an MIT licence. The frontier stayed where it was. What moved was the floor: the cheapest usable option in every category got better and cheaper at once, and self-hosting closed on the hosted tier again.

    1. Mon 27 Jul Copilot gains Word, Excel and PowerPoint agents

      Picking a model per task moved out of admin settings and into the hands of office users.

    2. Wed 29 Jul Gemini 3.6 Flash cuts output tokens by 17 per cent

      Shorter answers at a lower price per token, which saves on the bill and on the reading.

    3. Thu 30 Jul OpenAI cuts GPT-5.6 Luna by 80 per cent

      The single largest move of the week, and the one that dates every cost model written before it.

    4. Fri 31 Jul DeepSeek ships a new V4 Flash under an MIT licence

      Open weights a team can host itself, closing again on the hosted frontier.

    Key takeaway The price of running AI fell much faster than its capability rose. An 80 per cent cut on OpenAI's cheap tier, a cheaper and shorter-answering Gemini Flash, and MIT-licensed weights from DeepSeek all landed inside five days, while no frontier model got meaningfully better. Work that was too expensive to automate last month is affordable this month at the same quality.
