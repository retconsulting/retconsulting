Every setting, one tap away

The readout floats over the viewfinder. Tap a cell and its control opens at the screen edge opposite the shutter. Tap the cell again and you are back to the picture.

Shutter speed, on a dial

Turn the dial to set the exposure time. Turn it back to the red A and automatic exposure takes over.

  • The dial is engraved from 1/8000 to 4 seconds. Available speeds depend on the camera.
  • Auto shutter cap sets the longest exposure that automatic can use.
  • All auto returns every control to automatic in one tap.

Sensitivity, on its own dial

The ISO cell opens a second dial. Set a manual value, or turn to A and leave the choice to the camera.

  • Exposure programs: Auto, Tv, ISO and Av.
  • Haptic feedback in four strengths: Off, Light, Medium and Firm.
  • Mechanical sounds for dials and rails, on or off.

Lenses and simulated depth

The Lens card holds the focal lengths and the aperture ring. Each camera module has a fixed physical aperture, so another f-number is a simulated depth effect for portraits.

  • The focal selector reads in millimetres.
  • The cell says LENS or SIMULATED, so you know which aperture you see.
  • Simulated depth needs depth data and HEIF.

The meter, in words

With ISO or shutter set by hand, the EV cell on iPhone reads ON TARGET, UNDER or OVER, with the difference in stops.

  • Drag up or down on the viewfinder to change EV by 1/3 stop.
  • Touch and hold the EV cell to lock exposure.
  • On iPhone Duo, the cell shows AUTO, MANUAL or AE LOCK.

White balance, your way

Leave it automatic, lock it, choose a preset, or drag to the Kelvin value you want.

  • Five presets, one tap each.
  • A Kelvin slider from 2000 K to 12000 K.
  • Lock WB holds the current balance.

Autofocus, or the focus ring

Tap the viewfinder to focus and meter on that point. Switch to MF and drag the focus ring, on lenses with manual focus.

  • Touch and hold the viewfinder to lock focus and exposure.
  • A loupe magnifies the focus point as you drag.
  • Focus peaking marks sharp edges in red, green, blue, white or amber.

Browse the canisters and pick a look. The viewfinder shows it as you frame, so you judge the picture before you take it. NATIVE uses no film at all.

Viewfinder instruments

Turn on what the picture needs, from the Viewfinder panel, and leave the rest off.

Your photos stay on your iPhone

RAW, HEIF or both

Choose the files each shot saves. On supported hardware, the RAW file (DNG) stays free of the Dials film treatment, and the HEIF photo carries the film.

Every shot keeps its settings

The gallery shows the film, the effects and the camera settings of each photo, and switches between its RAW and HEIF versions.

Nothing to sign in to

Taking a photo sends nothing to RET Consulting. Dials has no account, no advertising and no third-party analytics. With your permission, it adds your photos to Apple Photos. Read the privacy policy.

Questions

When does Dials come out?

Soon, on the App Store. The date, the price and the countries are not announced yet.

Is Dials free?

No. Dials will be a paid app. The price will be on its App Store page at launch.

Does Dials change my lens aperture?

No. Each iPhone camera module has a fixed physical aperture. Portrait simulation is a software effect that uses depth data.

Does a film touch my RAW file?

On supported hardware, no. The RAW file (DNG) stays free of the Dials film treatment. The HEIF photo receives the film.

Where do my photos go?

They stay in the Dials gallery on your iPhone, and in Apple Photos if you allow it. Taking a photo sends nothing to RET Consulting. Privacy policy.

Is the app translated?

Not yet. The app interface is in English.

Were the pictures here taken with Dials?

No. They are demonstration photographs. The screens are native captures of the app, and the film examples come from its film engine. Credits.
