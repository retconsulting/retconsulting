Dials privacy policy

Updated · 9 November 2026

Dials processes your photos on your iPhone. Taking a photo does not send the image or your camera settings to RET Consulting. You can take photos without a Dials account.

Publisher and contact

RET Consulting SRL, a Belgian limited liability company, publishes Dials. Its registered office and company number appear in its legal notice. RET Consulting is the controller for support enquiries and the beta feedback it receives. Write to retbaz@retconsulting.be about your data or this policy.

Camera, motion and storage

Camera access supplies the viewfinder image and lets you take a photo. On supported cameras, Dials uses the position of a subject or face to adjust focus and exposure. It does not identify people or create face profiles. A portrait capture can include depth information for the portrait effect.

The horizon level uses device motion on your iPhone. Dials checks available storage before saving a photo and can show a low-storage warning. Dials uses these readings on the device. Dials does not request microphone or location access.

Dials keeps capture files, previews and details in its own gallery. Details can include the time, camera model, lens, exposure and selected film. It saves preferences and custom film settings locally. Dials contains no advertising or third-party analytics service and does not track you across apps or websites.

Saving to Photos and sharing

With your permission, Dials adds captures to Apple Photos. This permission does not grant access to browse your existing photo library. If you use iCloud Photos, Apple synchronises the Photos copies according to your settings.

Sharing opens the system share sheet. You choose the recipient or service. The file may include capture details and, for some portraits, depth information. Review the file before sending it. The selected service handles the copy under its own terms.

Keep or delete a photo

To remove a local capture, delete it in the Dials gallery. Handle its Photos copy in Apple Photos and any shared copies at their destination. Deleting the app removes its local gallery and preferences. Offloading the app can keep its data.

Dials asks iOS to exclude its gallery folder from device backups. Before deleting Dials or changing iPhones, verify the photos you want to keep in Apple Photos or export them. You can change camera and Photos permissions in iPhone Settings and turn off the horizon level in Dials.

Diagnostics and TestFlight

Dials uses device logs to help diagnose capture and performance issues. If you test a beta using TestFlight, Apple automatically collects and shares crash and usage information with RET Consulting. This can include build, device and session details. Your name and email may be visible depending on how you joined. Apple also shares the feedback or screenshots you choose to send. RET Consulting uses this information in its legitimate interest in investigating faults and improving Dials. It keeps any copies of individual reports it makes for as long as needed to investigate and resolve the reported issue. Apple sets its own retention periods. You can leave the beta in TestFlight. If you allow it in your device settings (Share With App Developers), Apple also shares crash and usage data from the App Store version with RET Consulting. Apple shares this data in a form that does not identify you. You can turn this sharing off at any time in Settings, Privacy & Security, Analytics & Improvements. Apple’s App Analytics privacy notice. Website and support

You can describe a camera problem by email without attaching a photo. If you attach an image or a report, RET Consulting receives the copy you send.

Your rights

You can view or delete your photos in the Dials gallery. RET Consulting cannot retrieve them from your iPhone. If your request concerns a support email or a TestFlight report received by RET Consulting, write to retbaz@retconsulting.be. How to exercise your rights.
